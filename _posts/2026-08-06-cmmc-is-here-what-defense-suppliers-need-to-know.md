For years, CMMC felt like a storm that was always "coming next year." That era is over. The Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification program is finalized, and CMMC requirements are now flowing into DoD solicitations. If your company builds parts, software, or services that touch the defense supply chain — including aerospace and space programs — this affects you, whether you sell to the government directly or sit three tiers down under a prime.

## The short version

CMMC is the Department of Defense's way of verifying that contractors actually implement the cybersecurity requirements they've been agreeing to in their contracts for years. It has three levels:

- **Level 1 — Basic.** For companies that handle **Federal Contract Information (FCI)**: information provided by or generated for the government under contract that isn't intended for public release. Requires 15 basic safeguarding practices and an annual **self-assessment** with an executive affirmation.
- **Level 2 — Advanced.** For companies that handle **Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI)** — think drawings, specifications, technical data marked for limited distribution. Requires all **110 security requirements of NIST SP 800-171**. Most contracts at this level require a **third-party assessment** by an authorized assessor (a C3PAO) every three years; a smaller set allows self-assessment.
- **Level 3 — Expert.** For companies supporting the most critical programs, adding selected requirements from NIST SP 800-172 and a government-led assessment. Most suppliers will never need this.

The rollout is **phased over several years**, but that's a trap if it makes you relax: primes are already pushing requirements down to their subcontractors ahead of schedule, because they can't win work with an unready supply chain.

## Why this catches manufacturers off guard

Most of the companies we work with are excellent at making things and lean on staffing everywhere else. The pattern we see over and over:

1. **They underestimate scope.** CUI isn't just in some locked file share — it's in email, in the ERP, on the shop-floor machine that displays drawings, in the backup system, sometimes at a machine shop partner.
2. **They assume IT has it handled.** NIST 800-171 is 110 requirements spanning policy, physical security, HR practices, and incident response — not just firewalls. A great IT provider can still leave half the requirements unowned.
3. **They start too late.** Real implementations take **12–18 months** for a typical small or mid-size manufacturer. If you start when the solicitation drops, you've already lost the award.

## What a passing posture actually requires

A few pieces matter more than everything else:

- **A defined scope.** Where does FCI/CUI live, flow, and get stored? Shrinking that footprint (enclaves, dedicated systems) is often the single biggest cost saver.
- **A System Security Plan (SSP).** The document that describes how each requirement is met. Assessors start here; so do primes when they evaluate you.
- **An honest gap assessment and SPRS score.** Level 2 companies must post a self-assessment score to the government's SPRS database. Inflated scores are now a serious liability — the Department of Justice has pursued contractors for misrepresenting compliance.
- **Evidence.** Policies nobody follows don't pass assessments. Screenshots, configurations, logs, and training records do.

> If you remember one thing: **CMMC is an evidence game, not a paperwork game.** Assessors verify that controls are implemented, not merely written down.

## First moves if you're starting now

1. **Figure out what you handle.** FCI only? You're a Level 1 shop and your lift is modest. CUI anywhere? Plan for Level 2.
2. **Ask your primes what they'll require and when.** Flow-down timing varies by program; your customers know their schedules.
3. **Get a gap assessment against NIST 800-171.** You want the bad news early, itemized, and priced — not discovered by an assessor.
4. **Scope before you spend.** Don't buy tools for the whole company if a small enclave can carry the CUI workload.
5. **Assign an owner.** Compliance without a named owner drifts. If you can't hire one, this is exactly what a vCISO is for.

CMMC rewards companies that start early and punishes companies that wait. The suppliers who treat it as a competitive advantage — "we're already certified, award us the work" — are the ones turning compliance cost into won contracts.
